My research training combines biomedical engineering with photonics. The projects below describe my experience and current interests; they are not a list of peer-reviewed publications.

<span class="st-eyebrow">MAY 2026 — PRESENT</span>
<h2 id="polarization">F-actin imaging by polarization fluorescence microscopy</h2>
**Leibniz IPHT, Microscopy Department, Jena**  
Supervisors: Prof. Rainer Heintzmann and Dr. Daniela Täuber.

This ongoing project uses the 2DPOLIM platform to study F-actin with polarization fluorescence imaging. My training focuses on the measurement workflow, interpretation of polarization-dependent signals, and the biological context of cytoskeletal imaging.

<span class="st-eyebrow">SEPTEMBER 2025 — APRIL 2026</span>
<h2 id="spectroscopy">Mid-IR spectroscopic imaging of RPE granules</h2>
**Leibniz IPHT, Microscopy Department, Jena**  
Supervisors: Prof. Rainer Heintzmann and Dr. Daniela Täuber.

I worked on PiF-IR measurements and hyperspectral analysis of retinal pigment epithelium granules, with guidance and training from the research team. PCA helped explore spectral heterogeneity in the acquired data. The work gave me experience connecting optical measurements with biological samples and checking the limits of an interpretation.

The reported 5 nm/pixel setting describes a sampling step, rather than a demonstrated spatial resolution. PCA scores describe variation in the dataset and do not directly establish chemical concentration.

<span class="st-eyebrow">JANUARY 2024 — SEPTEMBER 2025</span>
<h2 id="devices">BiFeO₃ memristor testing and MAGIC NOR logic</h2>
**Leibniz IPHT, Quantum Detection Department, Jena**  
Supervisors: Prof. Heidemarie Krüger and Dr. Nan Du.

Research training included BiFeO₃ memristor measurements, MAGIC NOR logic experiments, and endurance and retention tests. This experience motivates my interest in the interface between measurement systems, devices and future biomedical applications.
